- **Notifications — the feature the settings screen never had (branch `feat/notifications`, migration `20260924100000`), what to know:**

  **What was missing.** `NotificationsPage` let a company switch four types on and off and wrote them to `notification_settings`, and **no other code in the repository read that table**. No scheduler, no sender, no template: a TÜV expiry produced nothing. CLAUDE.md carried it under *Known defects*; that bullet is rewritten in this PR.

  **What ships.** `public.notification_deliveries` (the record of what was sent, and the dedupe rule), three `service_role`-only RPCs (`notification_candidates`, `notification_delivery_claim`, `notification_delivery_settle`), the pure decision in `supabase/functions/_shared/notification-core.ts`, four fixed German mails in `_shared/notification-mail.ts`, the Deno I/O in `_shared/notification-delivery.ts`, and the edge function `notify-expiries`. `tests/rls/notifications.sql` and 60 vitest cases cover it.

  **Nothing sends until a cron line is installed**, and that is the owner's call. Deploying the branch adds a function nobody calls.

  **The three decisions, made rather than assumed:**

  1. **"Soon" is 60 days for the TÜV and 7 for the trial.** Neither is new: `getTuevStatus()` in `src/types/car.ts` already turns the Fahrzeuge badge amber at 60 days, `TrialBanner` at `days <= 7`, and `20260502121504`'s own header documents both numbers for these very types. The mail and the screen must not disagree about the same car on the same day. They live once, as `TUEV_SOON_DAYS` and `TRIAL_SOON_DAYS`, and are *passed into* the SQL rather than restated there.
  2. **A re-notification is correct exactly when the underlying fact changes, and never because a night went by.** The dedupe key is `(company, type, target, period_key)`, and `period_key` is the date the notification is *about* — the car's `tuev_gueltig_bis`, or the company's `trial_end`. So a TÜV that expires and is never renewed is mailed once, not nightly; a TÜV that is renewed gets a new date, which is a new occurrence, so the company is warned again 60 days before it and told again if it lapses again; the warning and the expiry are separate occurrences of the same date, so a car warned at 60 days is still told on the day it passes; an extended trial is a new occurrence. It is a UNIQUE constraint, not a query, so two runs overlapping on one night cannot both claim.
  3. **A lapsed company is mailed, and a blocked one is not.** `approval_status = 'approved'` and `status IN ('active','trial')` are applied in SQL: a company that was never approved, was rejected, or was suspended, archived or deleted gets nothing, because suspension is an administrative block and mailing through it would undo it. The subscription terms of `get_active_company_id()` are deliberately **not** applied at that level — `trial_abgelaufen` is by definition a notification to a company whose access has just lapsed, and `trial_bald_ablaufend` is sent to a company in its trial, whose `subscription_status` is `'inactive'` because that is what signup writes. Instead both trial types require `NOT subscriptionIsActive(…)` in `notification-core.ts`. That single term is the paying-customer case.

  **The paying-customer case is the one that had to be got right.** `companies.status` stays `'trial'` for ever — signup writes it, `redeem_activation_code` writes the five `subscription_*` columns and never `status`. A job that judged the trial by `trial_end` alone would mail *"Ihre Testphase läuft bald ab"* to a customer who is paying. The browser has made that mistake twice (the customer's overview, the platform dashboard). `src/utils/notificationSelection.parity.test.ts` imports `subscriptionIsActive` from **both** `src/utils/planState.ts` and `_shared/notification-core.ts` and pins them to the same answers over twelve cases, and the same file walks the TÜV window against `getTuevStatus()` day by day. A negative control was run: flipping the job's NaN branch fails two rows.

  **Claim before send, and never retry.** The job inserts the delivery row (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`), then sends, then settles. If the worker dies in between, the notification is lost and never repeated — deliberately, because a nightly duplicate is the worse failure. A lost one is visible as `mail_sent IS NULL`; a failed send as `mail_sent = false`. A company whose *recipients* could not be looked up is skipped entirely and nothing is claimed for it, so a transient GoTrue failure does not burn a TÜV warning.

  **Who receives it:** `selectActivationCodeRecipients`' company branch, called with no target address — the company's active `admin` profiles that are not platform staff, with a confirmed, undeleted, unbanned account, and nobody when there are none. The rule is not restated; it is one function.

  **The wording is fixed German in `_shared/notification-mail.ts` and is not editable from the panel.** Making it editable means a `mail.template.*` settings key, and a key means restating **`app_private.platform_settings_problem`** with every existing branch carried over character for character, plus a self-check re-asserting what the old branches refuse. That is a slice of its own. The five templates `email-layout.test.ts` pins to their exact bytes are untouched; the new mails go through the same layout.

  **A new edge function, and why it is not a route.** `notify-expiries` costs its line in `FUNCTION_ENV` (`supabase/functions/main/index.ts`) and its comment in `deploy/docker-compose.yml`; both are in this PR. It is not a route on `platform-staff` or `manage-users` because its caller is a **cron line holding the service key**, which has no `auth.uid()` — every platform RPC would refuse it — so a route would have meant adding a branch that skips those functions' own authorization. It is deliberately **not** in `PUBLIC_FUNCTIONS`, and because the router's check only proves a token this stack signed (**every signed-in customer holds one**), the function compares the bearer against `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` in constant time itself.

  **One thing the kit caught, and it is worth knowing why.** The first version of the migration gave `notification_deliveries` **neither** context trigger, reasoning that no client can write it — which is true, and is why the job pays nothing for them. `tests/rls/platform-contexts.sql` section 14 refused it: the rule is *every table whose policies name `get_active_company_id()` carries `refuse_read_only_context_write`*, keyed on the **policies**, not on today's grants. That is deliberate — the next person to add an INSERT policy here (the "zuletzt gemeldet" panel below is the obvious candidate) will not re-derive the argument, and without the triggers their write would pass in a view context and leave no audit row in a support session. Both are installed now, because `tests/rls/support-write-audit.sql` section 1 fails a table carrying one without the other in either direction. The alternative — dropping `get_active_company_id()` from the SELECT policy to leave the rule's scope — was rejected: it is only right if a company should never read its own delivery history, and the opposite is the likely future. Section 4b of `tests/rls/notifications.sql` is the new check that the pair does **not** arm for the job: `service_role`'s JWT carries no `sub`, so the refusal trigger returns on its first branch, and without that check the pair would have put one audit row on every notification, every night, with no actor.

  **Two things left over, both small:** nothing in the product *reads* `notification_deliveries` yet (the SELECT policy is the tenant shape, so a "zuletzt gemeldet" panel on the Notifications screen is a UI-only follow-up — and it now has both triggers waiting for it), and `cars.status` is deliberately not filtered on — the column exists in production but has no `CREATE TABLE` in the migration chain, so its domain is unknown; the dedupe bounds an out-of-service car to one mail per expiry date.

- **One subscription model (plan 3.F step U2), what to know.** `20260919170000_subscription_invoice_coverage.sql`. U1 left invoices affecting nothing because the owner had not given a grace period; he gave it on 17.09.2026, and U2 is those three rules. Read this before U3.
  1. **Paying an invoice extends access.** The paid invoice records coverage for the calendar month it names, and the recompute derives the company's state from it.
  2. **Grace: two business days after the due date, then access closes.** Business days so that a Friday due date closes on the Tuesday and not on the Sunday.
  3. **Cancelling an invoice withdraws the coverage it granted**, and keeps the invoice, the payment and the trail row exactly as they were.
  - **The period an invoice covers is the calendar month it names, and that is the only period information there is.** `subscription_invoices` carries `invoice_month`, `invoice_year` and `UNIQUE (company_id, invoice_month, invoice_year)` (`20260607125649:150-169`) and no `period_start`, `period_end` or `service_from`.   - **"Due" is `subscription_invoices.due_date`, `date NOT NULL` (`20260607125649:161`), and no due *timestamp* was invented.** `GenerateInvoicesModal.tsx:78` writes it as `min(billing_day, 28)` of the invoiced month, so **the due date sits inside the month being invoiced, never after it** — which is why the grace has to be about an *unpaid* invoice, and why an unpaid invoice has to grant until it: a paid one already covers its whole month, which ends long after its own due date. `InvoiceDetailModal.tsx:41` and `SubscriptionBillingPage.tsx:196` read the column the same way. A `date` has no time of day, so the close is midnight Europe/Berlin and the customer keeps the whole of the due day and of each business day counted after it.

## Gotchas learned the hard way

- **ridy is off limits.** Its deploys reset its Caddyfile. The guard restored our section on both of ridy's deploys on 14.09.2026 (10:57 and 11:11 CEST).
- **`gh pr merge --delete-branch` switches the local checkout to the local base branch,** which may be stale. Pull right after.
- **`workflow_dispatch` workflows only become runnable once the file is on the default branch** (`main`).
- **Quoting through `ssh '…'` turns `$[` into bash arithmetic expansion.** Send scripts with `ssh host bash -s <<'EOF'` instead.
- **Caddy accepts any `@name` as a named matcher,** so a template placeholder must be an invalid directive to fail closed.
- **The `Enter passphrase for key … id_ed25519` prompt is local to your machine.** Pressing Enter falls back to the server password.
- **GoTrue v2.170.0 only rate-limits when `GOTRUE_RATE_LIMIT_HEADER` is set;** otherwise it silently does nothing.
- **Playwright applies the config's `httpCredentials` to every request context, including `request.newContext()`.** A "visitor without a login" built that way silently sends the test login. Use Node's `fetch` for anonymous checks.
- **supabase-js strips the URL hash while it creates a session from a link.** Anything that needs `type=` or `error=` must read it before the client is created; see `authRedirect` in `src/lib/supabase.ts`.
- **A single-file bind mount whose file is missing becomes an empty directory.** Mailpit then cannot read `mailpit-relay.yml` and does not start, or starts without its UI login. `deploy.sh` checks both files before a staging release.
- **"No buffer space available" / `ERR_NO_BUFFER_SPACE` on the dev machine means it ran out of sockets,** not that the host is down. On 14.09.2026 the MyPublicWiFi service held 16,000 bound sockets, and e2e, git and ssh all failed until it let them go. It came back the same day, and six agents died after five hours with `ENOTFOUND`. Stopping the service is the only real fix.
- **Mailpit logs a relay only when it fails;** success is a debug line, so a quiet log is the good case. To see what Resend did with a mail, list `GET https://api.resend.com/emails` with production's key (pass the header on stdin with `curl -H @-`) and read `last_event`. Print only the fields you need: the list holds customers' addresses.
- **A `grep` that matches nothing ends a `set -euo pipefail` script silently** — the same trap as the `.env` reads in `deploy.sh`. It hid the result of the first relay test, which had in fact been delivered.
- **On Windows, vitest rewrites the golden `.snap` files with LF line endings.** The content is byte-identical and `git diff` is empty, but with `core.autocrlf=true` git lists them as modified. Restore them with `git checkout -- src/utils/__snapshots__/` and never commit them. A `.gitattributes` line `src/utils/__snapshots__/*.snap text eol=lf` would end the churn.
- **A release copies only part of `deploy/`.** `install_release` copies the compose file, `kong.yml`, `nginx-spa.conf`, `deploy.sh`, `init-db`, the functions, the migrations and `dist`. Any other script the host runs (`set-*.sh`, `otp-housekeeping.sh`) stays at the copy installed by hand, so reinstall it when it changes.
- **Supabase grants EXECUTE on every new function to PUBLIC, anon and authenticated.** A function meant for service_role only needs all three revoked; `20260911120000` missed anon. A guard written as `auth.email() != '…'` or `auth.uid() <> …` lets an anonymous caller through, because the comparison is NULL. For every SECURITY DEFINER function a PR adds, check `has_function_privilege('anon', …)`.
- **Never delete a PR's branch before `gh pr view N --json state` says MERGED.** On 15.09.2026 a merge failed on a GitHub TLS timeout, the script deleted the branch anyway, and GitHub closed PR #11 unmerged. Pushing the head commit back and `gh pr reopen` recovered it.
- **An inline `style` beats a Tailwind class.** Most screens set their layout in `style={{…}}`, which cannot change with the screen width. A class such as `hidden md:flex` next to an inline `display` hides nothing. On 15.09.2026 that kept the landing page two to four columns wide on phones. Anything that has to follow the viewport (grid columns, padding, display) belongs in classes, not in the style object.
- **The Workflow tool does not save an agent's worktree.** When a run is cut off, commit what is on disk (as `wip:`) before relaunching; a resumed run only replays agents that had finished.
- **Nothing creates a `user_profiles` row by trigger.** `auth.users` carries only `mark_pending_oauth_signup` and `sync_user_profile_email`; `public.handle_new_user` exists and nothing invokes it. Every profile is written by a signup RPC. Any code or test that assumes a profile appears on its own is wrong — and `tests/rls/oauth-signup.sql` creates that trigger in its own prelude, so the RLS kit cannot catch the assumption.
- **Production's compose reads the GoTrue signup switch as `DISABLE_SIGNUP`, not `GOTRUE_DISABLE_SIGNUP`.** Setting the prefixed name there changes nothing and reads as if it had.
- **A platform account cannot be hard-deleted once it has issued activation codes.** The delete fails with `23503` on `activation_codes_created_by_fkey`, and forcing it would destroy real history. Disable the staff row instead; that is what was done to `admin@yamobility.de` on staging.
- **`otp-request` answers 202 for the public purposes whatever happened**, including when the address has spent its five codes for the hour. That is deliberate — the endpoint must not reveal whether an address has an account — but it means "the mail never arrived" and "we refused to send it" look identical from outside. When a code does not turn up, count the hour's sends in `app_private.otp_send_events` before suspecting the mailer.
- **A local resolver that refuses a name is not a missing DNS record.** On the dev machine `nslookup mail-staging.elprofessor.eu` answered `Query refused` from a local resolver while the record was perfectly fine — it resolves through `1.1.1.1` and the site answers. Same family as the MyPublicWiFi socket problem below: check against a public resolver, or with `curl -w '%{remote_ip}'`, before reporting DNS as missing.
- **A PostgreSQL regular expression takes its greediness from its first quantifier** (manual 9.7.3.5), not per atom as in Perl or JavaScript. `update\s+companies\s+set(.*?);` is therefore greedy as a whole, because `\s+` comes first, and it runs to the *last* semicolon of a function body rather than the first. U1's `app_private.companies_set_clauses` has that shape. It is harmless for the checks that use it today, but it is not a pattern to copy. Bound a capture with a negated class instead, as `[^;]*` in `app_private.subscription_column_writes()` does.
- **An engine never calls `t(...)`; it names a key beside its German text.** The pure modules under `src/utils/` are golden-mastered and feed exports and audit rows, so a message keeps its German string and gains `messageKey` / `messageVars` (or `<field>Key` / `<field>Vars`); the screen renders `engineText(t, ...)` from `src/i18n/engineMessage.ts` and falls back to the German. Two traps: a German dictionary entry must render **exactly** what the engine already produced, or turning a key on rewords the German UI (which is why `engine.phase3.repair.noValidMove*` is English in `de.ts`); and a German string that something **matches on** gets no key at all — `warningCategories.classify()`, `evaluateManualCandidate`'s `blockingChecks`, and the persisted `manual_exception.reason`. `src/i18n/engineMessages.test.ts` reads the engines' source and fails the build on a key missing from either dictionary.
- **A Recharts `dataKey` is also the key on the datum, so translating one without the other empties the chart.** The Overview's 6-month trend and driver-performance charts read `dataKey={t('overview.kpi.umsatz')}` off objects keyed `Umsatz`, `Kosten`, `Gewinn`, `Gehälter` — correct in German and, in the English UI, four series resolving to keys no datum had, so **both charts drew nothing and reported no error**. Both sides now come from the same `t(...)` call, and `TREND_SERIES` in `overviewHelpers.ts` is the one place naming the trend series. The same shape is worth checking on any chart whose series label is translated. Note this is *not* the engine-message pattern next door: a series name has no second life — nothing matches, classifies or audits it — so it is a plain `t(...)`, not a German string carrying a key.
- **A screen label follows the UI language; a string something else reads does not.** The two are easy to confuse, and the test is what *else* consumes the string. Left German deliberately and worth knowing before "finishing" the conversion: `wageLabel` in `HistoricalReconciliationWizard` (drawn into the Stundennachweis PDF); `MONTH_NAMES` / `MONTHS_DE` in Abrechnung, Dokumente and `InvoiceDetailModal` (date formatting per `src/i18n/index.ts`, and the Abrechnung copy also builds the exported image's filename); `Woche N` in `IncomeManagement` (written into `income.description` and matched back with a regex); `ReceiptModal`'s category values and the `'Unsicher'` OCR prefixes; `SalaryDetailsModal`'s `description` / `payment_category`; `'Deutschland'` as a stored default. Each carries a comment saying so at its site.
- **The refusal sentence a platform screen shows is the database's, not the browser's.** `callPlatformRpc` throws `result.error`, a German sentence the RPC wrote; only the "answer was unrecognisable" fallback belongs to us. Translating that fallback alone would thread `t` through a dozen call sites to change the one message a user almost never sees, while every real refusal stayed German. If platform refusals should read in English, that is a server-side change to the RPCs' `error` text, not a screen change.
- **The platform panel moved to `/admin`.** `/super-admin/*` is a rewrite, not a second route table, and the in-app links still point at the old path on purpose. Slice 3.13 removes the rewrite and those links **together**.
