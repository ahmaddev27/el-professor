## 1. MAIN OBJECTIVE

FleetBook must generate monthly driver and vehicle plans based on the company's actual imported data, not arbitrary simulated amounts.

The most important inputs are:

1. Driver working hours from the **Drivers page**.
2. Driver salary and payment configuration from the **Drivers page**.
3. Daily Uber earnings imported into FleetBook.
4. Uber tips (`Trinkgeld`) for each driver.
5. Actual trip details, when available.
6. Company city and starting location.
7. Vehicle and driver availability.
8. Actual calendar month boundaries.

**The system must calculate a feasible plan from these inputs. It must not invent unrealistic fares, modify historical financial values, or create impossible shifts just to make the totals look correct.**

---

## 2. DRIVER SHIFTS MUST MATCH THE DRIVERS PAGE

Currently, the system creates shifts based on generation logic that may not match the individual driver's working hours or salary settings.

This must change.

For every driver, fetch the authoritative configuration from the Drivers page, using the driver's immutable UUID:

- Driver name and UUID.
- Employment or contract type.
- Monthly salary, if configured.
- Hourly wage, if configured.
- Revenue commission, if configured.
- Target or contracted working hours.
- Maximum permitted working hours.
- Availability and working days.
- Scheduled breaks.
- Leave, sickness and confirmed unavailability.
- Driver-specific working restrictions.
- Relevant company and tenant ID.

Do not assume every driver works the same number of hours.

Do not apply a universal 240–360-minute shift requirement when the driver's actual configured working arrangements require something else. Validate whether this range is a simulation preference, a business constraint, or a driver-specific requirement.

### Required calculations

For each driver and month, calculate:

- Contracted monthly hours.
- Scheduled hours.
- Actual recorded hours, if available.
- Remaining or excess hours.
- Number of shifts.
- Paid and unpaid breaks, where configured.
- Salary or wage amount.
- Commission amount, if applicable.
- Difference between expected and calculated compensation.

**Salary and hours must reconcile with the Drivers page.**

Do not automatically change a salary to fit generated shifts, or invent worked hours to justify an existing salary.

If the existing salary and hours settings cannot be reconciled, show an explicit discrepancy and prevent an unsupported payroll claim.

For simulation planning, optimize the proposed shifts within the real configuration. Keep planning results separate from actual payroll records.

## 3. EVERY SHIFT MUST START FROM THE COMPANY CITY

Every generated operational block must begin at a defined starting point inside the city where the company is based.

Read the company location from its actual tenant settings:

- Company city.
- Company address or authorized operating base.
- Latitude and longitude, if available.
- Company-specific operating area.
- Applicable start and return rules.

Do not hardcode Solingen or any other city.

### Required shift sequence

Example:

**Company base → First pickup → Passenger trip → Empty repositioning → Next passenger trip → ... → Shift end**

The first passenger pickup does not need to be inside the company city, but the operational shift must begin from the configured company starting location.

The drive from the base to the first pickup must:

- Be calculated using routing.
- Consume real scheduling time.
- Have its empty kilometers recorded.
- Not be counted as a paid passenger trip.
- Not be hidden or deleted from internal operations.

If the company requires return-to-base at shift end, apply this from company settings and include the necessary time. Do not invent a universal return requirement.

**A shift may not simply begin at a random pickup location in another city without accounting for the vehicle's travel from its actual starting point.**

## 4. USE REAL TRIPS AND REALISTIC PRICES

This is a critical correction.

Trip prices must be based on authentic imported trip data whenever such data exists.

For real trips, preserve the imported:

- Original trip ID.
- Original trip date.
- Pickup and drop-off time.
- Pickup and destination.
- Driver identity.
- Fare amount.
- Uber fees or adjustments when provided.
- Tip amount.
- Cancellation status and charge.
- Original source information.

Never inflate individual trip prices to force the system to reach a daily revenue target.

### Example

If an actual Uber trip was 8.70 €, it must remain 8.70 €.

Do not change it to 19.80 € simply because the generated day needs more revenue.

Do not create an invented trip and label it as an actual Uber trip.

### If Uber provides only daily earnings

If the import contains a daily financial total but not individual trips, the system cannot reconstruct real trips from that number alone.

In that situation:

- Preserve the Uber daily total as an authentic imported financial value.
- Mark all generated trip details as `SIMULIERT`.
- Do not classify the generated rides as real or historical.
- Do not claim the simulated trips independently prove the Uber earnings.
- Display the distinction between imported actuals and simulated planning estimates.

Use realistic fares only for clearly labeled simulations, with documented pricing assumptions and a transparent reconciliation gap.

**Never manipulate real-world records or invent evidence of actual rides.**

## 5. DAILY UBER EARNINGS × 30%, 31%, 32% OR 33%

The daily financial calculation must be based on the **daily amount imported from Uber**.

The allowed percentage options are:

- 30%
- 31%
- 32%
- 33%

These are percentages, not multipliers of 30–33 times.

For a selected percentage `p`:

`Calculated daily share = Imported Uber daily amount × p / 100`

For example:

- Imported Uber amount: 500.00 €
- Selected share: 32%
- Calculated amount: 160.00 €

**Important:** The percentage must have a clearly defined business meaning in the application: company share, driver share, commission or other configured financial category.

Do not silently assume it is the driver's salary or that it represents the total price of trips.

Before implementation, inspect the existing financial configuration and Uber import schema and determine:

- Is the imported daily amount gross or net?
- Does it include Uber's service fees?
- Are tips included or excluded?
- Does the percentage apply to fares only or another eligible base?
- Who receives the calculated percentage?
- How are refunds, adjustments and cancellations treated?

Use the company's actual configured rule; document it explicitly.

If the required business meaning or eligible calculation base is not configured, mark the calculation as `CONFIGURATION_REQUIRED` rather than inventing an answer.

### Percentage selection

Make the allowed percentage selectable through company settings.

If the simulation uses a randomly selected percentage, it must:

- Be one of exactly 30%, 31%, 32%, or 33%.
- Have a documented scope: per month or per day.
- Be reproducible with a Seed.
- Remain stable during regeneration with the same settings.
- Never modify the imported Uber source amounts.

### Reconciliation

Show, for each driver and day:

1. Uber imported amount.
2. Applied percentage.
3. Calculated share.
4. Imported tips.
5. Relevant adjustments.
6. Total according to the configured accounting formula.
7. Difference from the source or target, if applicable.

**Do not force the sum of fabricated trip fares to match the calculated share.** The financial share is a derived accounting amount, not automatically the gross fare total.

## 6. TRINKGELD MUST MATCH UBER EXACTLY

Tips are a strict requirement.

For each driver, the total `Trinkgeld` must match the imported Uber tips for the same reporting period, **to the exact cent**.

Where daily driver-level tips are available:

`SUM(Driver trip tips for day) = Uber imported driver tips for day`

Where only monthly driver-level tips are available:

`SUM(Driver trip tips for month) = Uber imported driver tips for month`

Do not pretend daily tip distribution is known if Uber supplies only the monthly amount.

### Rules

- No invented tips.
- No random tip increases.
- No rounding differences beyond the specified cent-level currency representation.
- No transfer of tips between different drivers.
- No duplication of tips across days.
- No tips on empty repositioning journeys.
- No automatic tips on canceled trips unless explicitly supported by source records.

Preserve the original Uber values.

If imported tips are not linked to individual trips, store the driver/day tip total independently. Do not fabricate per-trip allocations and describe them as actual tip records.

If a simulation requires allocating tips to synthetic trip rows, label the allocation clearly as simulated and keep the original authoritative total separately.

Any unexplained difference must fail financial validation.

## 7. EXACT CALENDAR MONTH — NOT UBER WEEKLY PERIODS

The monthly planner must use actual calendar boundaries.

Examples:

- September 2026: 01.09.2026–30.09.2026.
- October 2026: 01.10.2026–31.10.2026.
- February 2027: 01.02.2027–28.02.2027.
- February 2028: 01.02.2028–29.02.2028.

Uber may organize imported statements into four or five weekly periods.

**These weekly periods must never determine the boundaries of FleetBook's monthly report.**

Instead, import the source periods and allocate their dated transactions into the selected calendar month according to the actual transaction dates and the configured accounting date rules.

A calendar month must:

- Begin on day 1.
- End on its actual last day.
- Include zero-activity days.
- Correctly handle midnight crossings.
- Correctly handle `Europe/Berlin` or the company's configured time zone.
- Avoid duplicate transactions at weekly and monthly boundaries.
- Avoid omitting transactions because they belong to an overlapping Uber statement week.

Do not simply divide a weekly aggregate across days without trustworthy date-level information.

If the source contains only a weekly total and daily amounts cannot be established, mark the daily breakdown as unavailable instead of inventing it.

## 8. AUTOMATIC EMPTY-DRIVE REPOSITIONING

Keep the Round 6 `REPOSITIONING` architecture.

Between two passenger trips:

`A → B` = passenger trip

`B → C` = empty repositioning

`C → D` = passenger trip

The internal repositioning drive must:

- Remain in the operational system.
- Use valid coordinates.
- Use a road routing engine.
- Have a realistic duration and distance.
- Be assigned to the correct driver and vehicle.
- Be included in working-time planning as appropriate.
- Never be counted as a passenger fare.
- Never be deleted from the internal movement history solely to hide routing problems.

Prefer short transitions of 2–5 minutes where feasible, but never force longer journeys into that limit.

The next ride must not start until the vehicle can physically reach its pickup location.

Also include the necessary trip preparation and configured safety margin.

## 9. REALISTIC SHIFT AND SALARY RECONCILIATION

The monthly planning engine must jointly optimize:

- Individual driver hours.
- Available driver days.
- Contract and salary configuration.
- Available vehicles.
- Actual Uber ride assignments.
- Realistic routing.
- Working-time and break constraints.
- Company base location.

Examples of forbidden behavior:

- Adding fake shifts to justify a fixed salary.
- Changing real fares to satisfy a percentage target.

- Changing real trip driver assignments without authoritative evidence.
- Ignoring travel between pickups.
- Using hours from the wrong month.
- Ignoring leave or maintenance.

If the input data cannot produce a fully consistent plan, provide an explicit reconciliation report instead of fabricating missing facts.
